One group, shifting dynamics through speed

    WHAT TO LOOK FOR

    A formation is only part of the story.

    Below the passing threshold, the group mixes. Riders with spare exposed capacity move up the flanks while the front rider swings off and drifts back: convective reordering and high Cx*.

    At threshold, the line forms. When moving up into the wind would exceed a rider's capacity, they stay on the wheel. Riders seek a free wheel rather than ride abreast, so the group narrows and lengthens and Cx* falls: the stretched phase, S.

    Past the sheltered limit, the group breaks. A rider who cannot hold the wheel lets a gap open. With wheel-spacing drafting the gap costs shelter, so it grows. Riders stuck behind, unable to come round, form a sub-peloton that may reorganise, chase or fragment.

    MODEL NOTES & SOURCE

    Grounded in the paper. Simplified for exploration.

    What is measured?

    Every simulated second, each rider's local ahead-count is recorded: another rider is ahead if more than 1 m forward and less than 1 m away laterally. As in the authors' NetLogo code, X is the number of riders whose ahead-count changed since the previous second. X* = X × 12/N, Cx* = 1 + ln(1 + X*), CxP* = PCR × Cx*. Group PCR is the mean of PCRi = Pfront × d / MSOi with a fixed d. Normally Pfront is the power to ride the default pace, as in the authors' NetLogo code (where the pace follows a schedule); optionally, the lead group's front rider's actual speed.

    Phase labels follow Section 2.8: PCR is smoothed with a 5-tick centred mean; ΔPCR is its first difference; ε = 0.10 × sd(ΔPCR) over the run so far; then, in order, PCR > 1 → Dcp; high Cx* with acceleration/deceleration → Da/Dd; low Cx* → S, Sf (decelerating, mid PCR) or Cs; high Cx* → Cch or Ccl; otherwise Transitional. Thresholds are the paper's Scratch-reference values. Because the paper calibrated these on a 12-rider empirical race, labels for very different settings should be read cautiously.

    Direction of change (ΔPCR). ΔPCR is how much the group PCR changed from one second to the next. Positive means effort is rising (the pace is going up, or the road is steeper); negative means it is easing. The paper uses it to tell phases apart that look the same in PCR and Cx* alone: with a lot of reshuffling, a rising PCR gives Da (surging) and a falling one Dd (easing off); a strung-out line whose PCR is falling through the middle range gives Sf (strung out and tiring) rather than S. Because PCR flickers from second to second, the label uses a smoothed ΔPCR: PCR is first averaged over 5 s centred on each second (so labels appear 2 s late), and a change counts as rising or falling only if it is larger than ε, one tenth of the typical size of ΔPCR so far in the run (its standard deviation). Smaller changes count as steady. The ΔPCR card shows the raw one-second change.

    How the stretched phase (S, Sf) is identified. The paper defines S by PCR and Cx* alone: Cx* at most 1.693 and PCR at least 0.738 (Section 2.8). In words, it describes S as a single-file or elongated formation. The lab adds one check on the formation itself. When the lead group is stretched and its PCR is above 0.488, the peloton is labelled S, even if some reshuffling keeps Cx* above 1.693. If the lead group's PCR is below 0.738 and PCR is falling, the label is Sf instead. A group counts as stretched when its longest single-file line holds at least 4 riders and at least 40% of the group. In a single-file line, each rider is directly behind the one ahead: no more than 0.5 m to the side, no more than 3 m back, and with nobody alongside. Riders swinging off after a pull are not counted.

    How do the cyclists move?

    Relation to the NetLogo model. This browser model is written from scratch rather than ported line by line, but it is largely based on the authors' NetLogo model. These follow it closely: the PCR (peloton convergence ratio: the power the front rider needs × d ÷ a rider's MSO, its maximal sustainable output) and the braid-complexity measures: ahead-counts (how many riders each rider has ahead of it), X (how many riders' ahead-counts changed in the last second), Cx* (positional complexity, 1 + ln(1 + 12X/N), normalised to 12 riders, where N is the number of riders) and CxP* (PCR × Cx*). Also the power parameters, PCR from the default pace with a fixed d (the drafting coefficient: the share of exposed power a sheltered rider needs), Olds drafting with NetLogo's draft cones, and its cohesion and separation rules. Riders here have a bike length, momentum and an energy budget, and the rules below for pulling, moving up, passing, gap closing and tactical waiting are this model's own. The authors adjusted them while comparing the model with the NetLogo model and with what is seen in races.

    Power and shelter. Power to ride exposed at speed v is ½ρCdA v³ + Crrmg cosθ v + mg sinθ v, with the NetLogo parameters reported in the paper: drag area CdA 0.32 m², rolling-resistance coefficient Crr 0.004, air density ρ 1.226 kg/m³, rider and bike mass m 75 kg, gravity g, road gradient angle θ, and no wind. A sheltered rider needs d times that power. Shelter follows NetLogo's calculate-draft. If more than one rider is ahead within the draft zone in a 60° cone, d follows Olds' formula (the paper's Eq. 5, d as a function of the wheel gap; intercept set by the d slider) from the nearest of them. Otherwise d comes from the nearest rider in a 10° cone, and if there is none, d = 1. Olds' spacing is the wheel gap, because riders here have a bike length; NetLogo's turtles are points.

    Cohesion, separation and the middle of the road. As in NetLogo, each rider is pulled toward the mean position of the riders within 20 m in a 140° cone ahead: 0.11 per second along the road, 0.07 across it. It is pushed away from riders within 1.5 m in a 240° cone, at 0.05 m/s per neighbour (the mean of NetLogo's random factor, without the noise). The forward pull is limited to 0.33 m/s per metre of room ahead, to the wheel followed and to the wheel in the rider's own lane, because speeding up here costs power. Every rider also drifts toward the middle of the road at 0.02 m/s, except when passing on the outside. A rider never drifts into the line directly behind a rider just ahead, and these lateral drifts pause during a lull (a quiet spell of tactical waiting, below).

    Drafting and gap closing. Everyone wants to draft. A rider in the wind steers to within 0.15 m of its wheel's line, inside the 10° draft cone. A rider who already has shelter holds its line. A rider counts as losing a wheel when a gap of more than 2 m keeps opening for 1 s. Riders behind react as soon as they see a gap opening ahead of their wheel: they come round and close it if that costs less than 90 % of their remaining W′ (W-prime: a rider's finite anaerobic reserve, the energy available for efforts above its MSO; it drains above MSO and refills below it), otherwise they stay behind and go with it. W′ is part of this model's rider rules, not of the paper's analysis: PCR, Cx*, CxP* and the phase labels do not use it. A trailing group chases the group ahead. Within 80 m its leader rides hard to bridge, at the tail speed + 0.4 × gap (1–3 m/s faster), as far as its power allows. Further back it chases in an organised way, at any distance, as long as it has the capacity: its chase speed must be above the speed of the group ahead. The chase speed is what its stronger half can hold sharing the front (each spending 1/k of the time exposed), but no faster than its weakest member can follow in shelter. While chasing, riders take 8–12 s turns and swing off early only below 15 % W′. Groups separate when a wheel gap exceeds 6 m for 2 s, and rejoin under 3 m.

    Pulls. Each group's front rider aims for the requested pace for 15–30 s, up to three times longer during a lull, or until it cannot hold the pace or its W′ falls below 70 % (40 % near threshold, i.e. when the group's PCR approaches the paper's high threshold of 0.738, where passing becomes very hard). It then swings off into a free corridor 1.4 m to the side and drifts back. Below threshold it slips into a free sheltered slot within the first 10 riders; at threshold it drifts further back (see "After a pull, rejoin").

    Urge to move up. The Urge slider only sets how often a rider who is able to move up actually tries. This rule is not discussed in the paper; it is part of this model's own movement rules. A rider's chance of starting a move each second is 0.5 × urge × (a + b) × lull factor (the tactical-waiting damping, below). In this formula, a = (0.25 + 0.6 × its rank from the front, 0 to 1) × its capacity, and b adds up to 0.6 for a rider in the back third, however weak, as long as it has some capacity (defined under Moving up). At the default 1.2, a rider at the back with full capacity tries about every 1.2 s once it has room; at 0.3 it tries about every 5 s; at 0, nobody moves up. Whether a rider is able to move up (room to step out, capacity, the passing rule) does not depend on the slider. With tactical waiting on, quiet spells limit moves more than the urge does. In 16-rider runs at 32 km/h, moves per rider per minute rise from about 0.6 at urge 0.3 to 1.6 at urge 3 (1.6 to 3.8 without tactical waiting).

    Moving up. A rider's capacity runs from 1 at compact-static speeds (its ideal sheltered PCRi, the rider's own PCR when sheltered at the group's speed, ≤ 0.488) to 0 at PCRi = d. It sets how many riders the rider may pass in the wind before tucking in: up to 10, none at PCRi = d. A move needs room beside the rider to step out, toward the nearer flank if possible. A rider boxed in on both sides waits. Riding in the wind faster than the group must be sustainable (exposed power at the group's speed + 0.8 m/s ≤ MSO). In a relaxed bunch (group PCR well below that threshold, about 0.55 or less), a rider with capacity left may instead use its W′, if the pass costs less than half of what remains. A rider passing on the outside angles in gradually (at most 0.25 m/s sideways), keeping 0.8 m outside the riders it passes. It then tucks into a slot just ahead on a diagonal. A rider boxed in during a move eases off by only 0.15 m/s to slip out, and gives up after 3 s. Riders also ease back through the bunch now and then, and drift back after a pull. These, the pulls and the moves up are what produce the convective reordering.

    Getting back in. In a relaxed bunch, a rider further behind the group's head than the bunch's compact length (about 2.2 m × √N, with N the riders in the group, so about 12 m for 30 riders) is off the back; this happens after a surge or a mishap has strung the bunch out. Such riders try to get back in even if it takes them close to their threshold, while the riders at the front just hold the pace. They move up whatever the lull, with an urge that grows with how far back they are. They may pass as many riders as it takes, on W′ down to 25 %, and close up in their own lane when there is room, at up to 1.2 m/s above the group's speed. After a 30 s surge to 42 km/h, a 30-rider bunch back at 32 km/h closes up again within about 45 s.

    Tactical waiting. At easy pace (group PCR at pace ≤ 0.45, fading out by 0.55, about 36 km/h for the Scratch field, i.e. the riders' MSOs from the paper's Scratch-race data) a bunch tends to hold position until someone makes a move. Above that, a relaxed bunch keeps mixing. The lead group has an activity level that fades over about 10 s. About once every 40 s, a rider with spare capacity makes an unprompted move; this lifts activity and sets off a burst of follow-on moves, which then dies away. Moving up, easing back and the lateral drifts are scaled down when activity is low.

    Road, starting grid and energy. The road is 15 m wide. Up to 30 riders start in two staggered rows, half the field in each, side by side 1.1 m apart and centred on the road; larger fields start spread across the road's whole width, in as few rows as that allows. Power is capped at MSO × (1 + √(W′ remaining / W′ capacity)), with a W′ capacity of 60 J per watt of MSO. W′ drains above MSO and recovers below it, and speed follows the power balance.

    Limits

    No wind, cornering, team tactics or calibrated fatigue physiology. Several values are teaching assumptions rather than measurements: the 0.8 m/s passing margin, the W′ rules, pull lengths, the move-up and tactical-waiting rates, the centre-bias and angling rates, and the decision rules. The NetLogo cone and zone sizes (20 m / 140°, 1.5 m / 240°, 60° / 10°) are taken from the authors' code comments and may differ from the interface values used in particular runs. Random seed. Each reset draws a new random seed: a number that fixes every random choice in a run. It is not a starting position: the starting grid is always the same (two staggered rows, or more rows across the road above 30 riders). The seed decides which MSO each rider gets when MSOs are drawn at random, and so who starts where on the grid (with the 12 Scratch riders, the default, every rider's MSO is used once, in a fixed order). It also decides each rider's preferred spot across the road, pull lengths, where riders rejoin after a pull, when riders try to move up, and the pace schedule of Random fluctuations. The seed is recorded in the exports; with the same seed and settings a run is reproducible. All data are synthetic, and nothing leaves the browser until exported.
